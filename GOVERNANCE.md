# Governance

## Roles

- **Contributor** — proposes a source, record, correction, or improvement.
- **Researcher** — structures assertions and records source provenance.
- **Reviewer** — checks evidence, classification, conflicts, and safety.
- **Editor** — publishes changes, resolves disputes, and manages releases.
- **Maintainer** — manages repository access, automation, and project policy.

One person may hold several roles, but an autonomous agent may never be the final
reviewer or merger of its own work.

## Decision principles

- Evidence quality takes priority over speed or coverage.
- Editorial methodology applies equally to sponsors and non-sponsors.
- Sourced history is corrected through superseding records, not silent deletion.
- Material policy or taxonomy changes are documented through pull requests.
- High-risk claims receive a second human review.

## High-risk changes

The following require two human approvals:

- structural market-gap findings;
- negative, disputed, or legally sensitive company information;
- impact and performance figures;
- named deployments supported only indirectly;
- changes to verification rules;
- changes that expose more precise infrastructure geography; and
- enabling an autonomous agent to publish or merge.

The last item is prohibited in the current phase and would require a public
governance change.

## Conflicts of interest

Researchers and reviewers disclose material employment, investment, advisory,
commercial, or personal relationships. Conflicted reviewers may clarify facts
but should not make the sole publication decision.

## Appeals

An affected organisation or contributor may open a correction request with
evidence. An editor who did not make the original disputed decision should review
the appeal where practicable. The resolution and supporting reasoning remain in
the public change history.
