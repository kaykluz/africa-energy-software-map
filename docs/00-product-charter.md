# Product charter

## Definition

The Africa Energy Software Map is a living, searchable public record of the
software that runs African energy: what each product does, who built it, where
it is verifiably deployed, and the evidence behind every entry.

## Problem

Information about energy software is scattered across provider sites, utility
announcements, procurement records, programme databases, reports, repositories,
accelerator cohorts, and informal sector knowledge. Existing directories rarely
join product capabilities to evidenced African deployments and source history.

## Phase 1 users

- Utilities and system operators finding products and implementation references
- Mini-grid, C&I, and utility-scale developers comparing operational tools
- Investors, lenders, and DFIs understanding markets and deployment evidence
- Founders identifying partners, competitors, and gaps
- Researchers, regulators, journalists, and advisers analysing digitalisation
- Providers claiming, correcting, and maintaining public records

## Core user questions

- What software exists for this part of the energy system?
- Where is it actually deployed?
- Who uses it?
- Is the deployment independently evidenced or only provider-claimed?
- How current is the information?
- What appears missing because of incomplete research, and what is structurally
  thin?

## Phase 1 scope

- African-built and global products used in Africa
- Commercial, proprietary, open-source, public, and hybrid systems
- Products, capabilities, deployments, organisations, sources, and assertions
- Utilities, C&I, mini-grid, off-grid, and utility-scale contexts
- Current, pilot, acquired, renamed, inactive, and historical records

## Phase 1 exclusions

- General directories of developers, EPCs, financiers, and advisers
- Comprehensive physical-asset mapping
- Precise non-public infrastructure coordinates
- Unmoderated editing
- Subjective vendor rankings
- Paid inclusion or verification
- Confidential transaction and project data

## Launch quality gate

The first public release should aim for approximately:

- 100 products
- 25 African countries
- 75–100 evidenced deployments
- 12–18 categories
- a source and last-checked date for every material published assertion

These are quality goals, not a completeness claim.

## Success measures

- Users can find a relevant evidenced deployment within three interactions.
- Provider claims and independent evidence are understood without reading the
  full methodology.
- Filtered views are shareable and exportable.
- Corrections are reviewable at field and assertion level.
- At least 90% of published active records remain within their review period.
- No autonomous process can publish without human review.
